I spent the past 10 days in Italy. I am not as tan as I thought I would be, but there was a lot of New York I needed to shed. After flying JFK to Rome and Rome to Bari, I carried my dehydrated self over to the car rental counter inside the airport.

I threw my carry-on into the trunk of a Fiat and drove towards the hills near Polignano a Mare. Puglia, in all her desolate sand colored glory, felt as if Death Valley was located in Northern California. Strange trees and cacti lined the winding roads. The Puglian people drove with abandon, and having driven in Italy before, I knew that the only thing that you need to know while on the road is to move over a lane if the person is quickly approaching behind you. The Italians enjoy long lunches and resting, but they have zero patience for getting to where they need to be. I guess the faster they get there, the sooner they can eat and rest. In Los Angeles fashion, I rolled the windows down and felt the hot and dry air whisper against my shoulders, and before the day ended, I needed to know what the Adriatic Sea felt like.

I spent four nights at Villa Torre Bianca. The owner of the villa, his gorgeous wife who seemed to only wear DÔEN, and their six children from California were also spending the summer there. Each child was named after a city in Italy, i.e. Como, Roma etc. The kitchen cooked them meals off menu, and the children—who had to have been under 10—hopped into a sprinter van daily to play in the sea while the parents downed prosecco at a beach club. In the evening, they would roam the grounds, play soccer on the grass with their local friends, and swim as the sun sets before filling their little bellies with spaghetti al pomodoro. I lied and read on a lounge chair as often as I could, praying my smoking and spritzes weren’t interrupting their family vacation. If they ever leave, I’d book the tower they stayed in and live like I owned the villa too.

Most memorable meals in Puglia:

L’Osteria di Chichibio
Osteria Perricci
& a wine tasting at Terre di San Vito

I have since become fascinated by recognizing certain notes from wine, and have decided to learn as much as I can on my own without becoming a sommelier. I plan on using this power to recommend excellent wines to order with dinner, ideally without being annoying. Considering I’m unemployed, becoming a faux sommelier seems like a reasonable thing to do.

I slept by 11pm every evening, and woke up at 7am by light alone. The landscape surrounding the villa reminded me of Spaghetti Westerns, so on one of those early evenings I threw on The Good, the Bad, and the Ugly. I watched this film many years ago, when dating a Mexican cowboy in Los Angeles.

The costume design by Carlo Simi, known for Once Upon a Time in America and Django, is excellent. A majority of the coats I own were inspired by Clint Eastwood’s style. And while the looks are great, it’s the score by Italian composer Ennio Morricone that really makes it potent.

One 28 Euro Ryan Air flight later, I landed in Venice and spent two nights at Ca Maria Adele. If there is one thing I am certain I am skilled at, it is choosing hotels (and traveling overall), so I knew to book water taxis prior because that is the only acceptable way to travel in Venice. The hotel is known for its decadent and ornate themed rooms, and they truly care about service. Upon docking you don't lift a finger, and are giving prosecco before you can ask. Venice was equally as hot as Puglia, and it seemed everyone there was over 60 years old and had that fabulous older Italian women art-centric sense of style (wears color-framed glasses and chunky jewelry with an unexpected hair color). Thankfully Venice was far less touristy than previous visits. If I had one recommendation it would be not to spend a single second in San Marco, and just walk until it gets quiet and there are chalkboard menus outside of restaurants written only in Italian. Ideally I’d have spent time visiting the other islands of Venice, but time was not on my side.

Most memorable meals in Venice:

La Zucca
Osteria il Milion
Campari Spritz(s) at Naranzaria

I spent the next two days up north in Cividale del Friuli, a charming town in the province of Udine. I saw one of my best friends get married to a man she loves very much and who loves her even more. They threw a wedding I’ll remember all my days. Not having attended many thus far, what I have come to realize is that there are three things that people seem to speak of and remember the most when it comes to a wedding.

The Food
The Timing
The Music

Everything else is secondary. And to be transparent, it doesn’t seem like anyone cares much about the flowers.

Now a vacation rule I tend to stick by and highly recommend:

On your last night or two of a vacation (obviously depending on budget) stay at the nicest hotel you can get your hands on. You leave your trip on such a high, along with a luxurious shower or bath before an international flight, and quality sheets to layabout in. I really splurged (before lay off) and booked one night at Hotel Cipriani.

The hotel, which exists in a 15th-century Palazzo, has wined and dined every celebrity you can think of, and houses the beautiful, talented, and rich during the Venice Film Festival and Biennale. Upon arriving by water taxi, I was greeted by James, a man from America that studied abroad in Venice nearly 30 years ago and never went back. I was once again handed a glass of cold prosecco, and upon checking in I was informed of a complementary upgrade for our night. Whether it was the one night stay, my Italian last name, or good fortune, they upgraded us to their signature suite. Though having just been a guest at a wedding a day before, I felt like I was on my honeymoon. Typically I would never recommend spending too much time at a hotel, but when you get upgraded to a 7k a night room, I felt it was reasonable to stay put.

I spent the evening floating in their heated salt water swimming pool, drank a cocktail titled the Buonanotte, which was basically a Cosmopolitan minus the triple sec with fresh ginger and served in a cup with the good type of ice, and was surrounded by so much antique marble I nearly turned evil.

As I left on a water taxi back to the airport and the pink hotel faded in the distance, I knew my mind would bookmark this memory and there would be no need to write in depth about it. Thank you to James and the handsome room service waiters for impeccable service and consideration. I will be doing what I can to make my way back, with or without an upgrade.

Takeaways:

Be kind
Get your money up
